Reproducing GDEM: Accuracy Is Not Transfer.

A 2025 experimental retrospective on Graph Distillation with Eigenbasis Matching (GDEM), graph Fourier signals, and a six-dataset reproduction: predictive accuracy aligned closely, but transfer across GNN backbones did not.

AidenResearch period: Dec 2025
Read the study ↗View reproduction source ↗
Central question

What does a close reproduction actually reproduce?

Node classification accuracy aligns closely with the published GDEM values. But when a distilled graph is evaluated using six GNN backbones, the agreement weakens. These are two separate empirical claims, not interchangeable measures of reproduction fidelity.

In brief

Reproducing GDEM (Graph Distillation with Eigenbasis Matching, ICML 2024) on six datasets matched the published node-classification accuracy closely: 17 dataset–budget comparisons with a mean absolute gap of 0.34 percentage points.

Transfer of the distilled graphs to other GNN backbones was less faithful, with a gap of −4.76 percentage points on Flickr.

A reproduction should state which property it reproduced: accuracy under the primary classifier did not imply cross-architecture transfer.

01 / Foundations

Why graphs carry more than node features

01

An image is sampled on a regular grid. A graph is an irregular set of entities joined by relationships: the graph's information exists in attributes, connectivity and the way neighborhoods interact.

For node classification, a graph can be written as G = (V, E, X, Y): V is the set of nodes, E the connections (possibly weighted or directed), X the node feature matrix, and Y the available labels. An identical set of node features can lead to different predictions when the edges change—because the edges determine which observations are relevant to each node. Community structure, bridges and heterophilous boundaries cannot be reconstructed from node counts alone. [5][13][16]

Node information
X

What an entity is

Attributes, learned embeddings, labels and initial observations attached to each node.

Edge information
A

Who interacts with whom

Adjacency, relation strengths, topology, bridges and whether connected nodes tend to agree.

Neighborhood information
AkX

What reaches a node

After k rounds, a message-passing model can incorporate information from nodes up to k hops away, subject to the chosen operators.

hv(ℓ+1) = UPDATEℓ(hv(ℓ), AGGℓ{ hu(ℓ) : u ∈ N(v) })Generic message-passing layer · aggregate neighbor states, then update the target state

Each GNN layer blends a new neighborhood into the representation. Early layers often capture local community membership, while deeper layers can access wider relational context. This is useful, but it does not mean information is guaranteed to improve—or degrade—monotonically with depth. Two distinct mechanisms are important:

DEPTH EFFECT 01

Over-smoothing

With repeated smoothing-style aggregation, neighboring node embeddings may become too similar to distinguish classes or communities. It is a risk for some architectures and depths, not a universal law of GNNs. [14]

DEPTH EFFECT 02

Over-squashing

Information from a rapidly expanding set of distant nodes can be compressed through a limited-dimensional bottleneck, losing dependencies even before representations become uniform. [15]

Key distinction

Graph distillation is not a cure for over-smoothing or over-squashing. Those are message-passing limitations. Distillation instead asks whether a much smaller training graph can preserve enough joint node–edge structure to train useful downstream models.

02 / Motivation

Why distill a graph at all?

02

On a large graph, training cost is not set by the number of labeled nodes alone. Message passing repeatedly touches edges, expands sampled neighborhoods and moves activations through the model.

Repeated model training—architecture search, hyperparameter sweeps, ablations and cross-backbone evaluation—multiplies these costs. The aim of graph dataset distillation (or condensation) is therefore to learn a compact synthetic training graph G′ with far fewer nodes than the original G, while retaining task performance when a model trained on G′ is evaluated on the original test graph. Distillation is not the same as simply dropping nodes

Sampling

Keep observed nodes/edges. Simple, but the remaining graph may omit rare neighborhoods and important bridges.

Coarsening

Aggregate nodes into supernodes while preserving selected structure. The reduced graph is tied to a chosen compression rule.

Distillation / condensation

Learn synthetic node features and, depending on the method, synthetic structure so downstream training still works.

Classic condensation objectives align gradients, training trajectories or representation distributions. They can inherit preferences from the supervision backbone. GDEM instead targets a shared structural coordinate system—the graph's spectral basis—while keeping a class-discrimination signal. This motivates its claim of portability across different GNN architectures, which is precisely what the reproduction tests. [1][2][3][4][17][22][23]

What success means

Not a miniature graph that looks visually identical to the original. The practical test is performance retention on the original task, ideally across multiple downstream GNNs and with measured training/serving cost. The 2025 study reports predictive fidelity and transfer; it does not reproduce an end-to-end runtime benchmark.

Where this sits in the literature. Graph condensation builds on dataset gradient matching and distribution matching in vision, adapts these ideas to graph neighborhoods, and includes alternatives such as gradient-free Bonsai, hyperbolic HyDRO and multi-scale BiMSGC. These are useful neighboring approaches, not methods experimentally benchmarked by this 2025 study. [12][17][18][19][20][22][23]

03 / Graph signal processing

From node neighborhoods to graph Fourier modes

03

Fourier analysis has a graph analogue. Instead of measuring oscillation across time or pixels, graph frequency measures how sharply a signal changes across connected nodes. The graph Laplacian supplies the coordinate system. [11][16][21]

For a simple undirected graph with adjacency A and degree matrix D, the normalized Laplacian is L = I − D−1/2 A D−1/2. Its eigendecomposition is L = UΛU⊤, where the columns of U are graph Fourier modes and Λ contains their associated frequencies. In this convention, normalized-Laplacian eigenvalues lie between 0 and 2.

L uk = λkuk   ·   x̂ = U⊤x   ·   x = Ux̂Eigenvectors = graph-frequency basis; x̂ = coefficients of a node signal in that basis

Low frequency (small λ) corresponds to smoothly varying values on connected nodes. It often captures community-scale patterns or homophily. High frequency (large λ) corresponds to rapid changes across edges, which can reflect noise or meaningful heterophilous labels, sharp boundaries and alternating structures. Treating all high-frequency variation as disposable noise would be a category mistake.

Graph total variation: a more precise view of “noise”

For the combinatorial Laplacian Lc = D − A, the Dirichlet energy of a node signal x is proportional to the squared difference across edges. Large energy means the signal changes strongly across neighbors. The normalized-Laplacian variant uses degree-weighted signals.

TV(x) = x⊤Lcx = ∑(i,j)∈E wij(xi − xj)²For undirected edges counted once. This quantity is graph-signal variation, not a calibrated measurement of random noise.
Low-frequency priority

Preserves smooth, cluster-consistent signal

Useful when neighboring nodes share labels (homophily). Too much exclusive low-pass emphasis can smooth away boundaries.

High-frequency preservation

Retains non-smooth relational detail

Useful when neighboring labels differ (heterophily) or a bridge carries class information. It can also amplify irrelevant variation.

The GDEM paper motivates a two-band selection: keep low-λ modes and high-λ modes. The middle frequencies are not explicitly retained by that truncated representation. The central trade-off is not “minimum versus maximum noise,” but which frequencies contain task-relevant information and how much spectral capacity a small synthetic graph can preserve. [1][4][8][9][10][11]

Connection to the 2025 reproduction

Several local Flickr configurations set ratio=1.0 in the eigenpair selection code, allocating all selected modes to the low-frequency band. That provides a concrete reason to investigate high-frequency under-coverage when interpreting the transfer gaps, but it is not proof of causation without a matched spectral-ratio ablation.

04 / Framing

From graph distillation to a reproducibility question

04

The background establishes what a condensed graph must retain: useful node signals and relational structure. This retrospective asks whether the 2025 GDEM implementation reproduced the published classification scores and the stronger claim of cross-architecture generalization.

Much of the literature supervises condensation through a particular model: gradients, training trajectories, or learned representations. These signals can encode the teacher's preferences, making the synthetic graph less portable to a different evaluator. GDEM, introduced by Liu, Bo and Shi at ICML 2024, seeks a more model-independent target by matching graph eigenbases and features rather than relying on a distillation GNN. [1][2][3][4]

Historical code inspection

The 2025 reproduction evaluated six datasets—Citeseer, Pubmed, Squirrel, ogbn-arxiv, Flickr and Reddit—with multiple node budgets. An independent public implementation remains available. The goal of this retrospective is to report the successful alignments without smoothing over the less successful transfer measurements.

05 / Method

How GDEM constructs a spectral training substitute

05

GDEM uses spectral information from the original graph to optimize the synthetic eigenvectors and node features. Unlike methods that match gradients from a particular distillation GNN, its main alignment signal is intended to describe structure that can be reused by different downstream architectures. Low and high selected Laplacian modes form the target basis; the eigenvalues themselves are carried from the original graph. The reproduction code exposes this allocation through eigen_k and ratio. The original objective has three terms: eigenbasis–feature matching (Le), a class-level discrimination loss (Ld) and an eigenbasis orthogonality penalty (Lo). The discrimination term is not simply a penalty for mixing low- and high-frequency bands; it compares class-level representations. [1]

After optimization, synthetic adjacency is reconstructed using selected real eigenvalues λk and learned eigenvectors u′k. This separates the distillation target from the downstream architecture; it does not guarantee equally strong results for every evaluator or graph regime.

A′ ≈ Σk=1…K (1 − λk) u′k u′k⊤Truncated reconstruction — Liu et al. (2024), Equation 10

A compact spectral representation cannot preserve every original edge or mid-frequency pattern. The paper establishes a restricted spectral approximation under its assumptions, not perfect preservation of all graph structure or universal transfer. [1][11][16]

06 / Setup

Experimental protocol

06

The 2025 implementation followed a preprocessing → synthetic feature initialization → eigenbasis distillation → downstream evaluation sequence. Its repository records a 16 GB GPU workflow, contrasting with the larger accelerator budget used in the published work. Mean and standard deviation of distillation accuracy are reported over ten runs in the repository; cross-architecture rows average six distinct evaluator models, not ten repeated seeds. [1]

PREDICTIVE TRACK

Distillation accuracy

Train a downstream node classifier using a synthetic graph and compare its mean test accuracy with the corresponding published budget. Seventeen reported configurations form the main matched set.

TRANSFER TRACK

Architecture portability

Train GCN, SGC, PPNP/APPNP, ChebNet, BernNet and GPR-GNN separately on the distilled representation. Compare the means only when the real compression ratios align.

The implementation loads eigenpairs, selects low/high modes by a configurable ratio, optimizes learnable synthetic eigenvectors/features, then reconstructs the synthetic adjacency for downstream evaluation. Because the code and original paper differ in some implementation details, the results are best described as a reproduction of the method family, not a byte-for-byte replication of the original training system.

Important implementation distinction

The 2025 agent.py uses an MSE-to-identity penalty on a normalized class-similarity matrix. The ICML 2024 paper defines Ld with within-class and between-class cosine terms. These objectives are related in intent but are not algebraically identical. This needs to remain visible when interpreting replication accuracy.

07 / Empirical finding 1

Distillation accuracy matches closely

07

Across 17 reported dataset–budget configurations, our mean absolute deviation from the paper is 0.34 percentage points; the median absolute deviation is 0.09 points.

The signed mean difference is −0.20 points. These are descriptive statistics over configuration-level point estimates, not a paired statistical equivalence test. Results are especially close on Pubmed, ogbn-arxiv and Reddit. Flickr has a persistent negative offset, and Squirrel at the largest reported budget is 1.45 points below the paper.

09 / Interpretation

What could explain the gap?

09

These observations suggest several plausible mechanisms, but the experiments were not designed as controlled interventions separating their contributions. The explanation must therefore stop at a set of testable hypotheses rather than assigning a single cause.

HYPOTHESIS 01

Frequency coverage

Flickr's recorded distillation configurations use ratio=1.0, selecting only the low-frequency side of the retained eigenbasis. On a graph with substantial non-smooth label structure, that choice could limit transfer to models emphasizing additional frequencies. This is a configuration observation, not a measured ablation.

HYPOTHESIS 02

Evaluation recipes

The cross-architecture script exposes backbone-specific tuning knobs but the report describes minimally tuned evaluation. Different polynomial orders, dropout and optimization could explain part of the observed deficit. The source also implements APPNP where the paper labels a PPNP evaluator; those names cannot be assumed to describe exactly identical evaluation procedures. Without matched ablations, we cannot quantify that contribution.

HYPOTHESIS 03

Objective mismatch

The category-discrimination surrogate in the local code differs from the paper's explicit cosine formulation. It may affect representation geometry or cross-model behavior, but that direction was not independently tested.

HYPOTHESIS 04

Resource and run variance

Hardware limits constrain feasible spectral dimension and search. Yet a 16 GB memory budget alone does not prove that the differences were caused by memory. We lack a matched hardware-only comparison and should avoid attributing gaps to GPU capacity.

The distinction that matters

A close single-backbone reproduction does not establish cross-backbone generalization fidelity. Both must be evaluated as independent claims.

For future research, the most informative next experiment would hold a dataset and synthetic-node budget fixed while varying low/high eigenvector allocation, class-discrimination loss and evaluator hyperparameters one at a time. The historical record does not contain that controlled follow-up.

10 / Closing analysis

What the reproduction establishes

10

GDEM's primary node-classification results proved highly reproducible in the recorded 2025 experiments. Its broader promise—model-agnostic transfer across six different GNNs—was less consistently reproduced.

This is not evidence that the original paper's algorithm fails. It is evidence that the observed reproduction is stronger on one axis than another. The article supports the reported 17-point accuracy agreement and the separate, weaker cross-backbone results; it does not infer a causal culprit from observational gaps.

The remaining limits are specific: budget labels required reconciliation, one code-level discrimination term differs from the published objective, cross-architecture hyperparameters were not tightly matched, and no confidence intervals or per-run paired results were available for transfer. Reported paper efficiency findings are background, not an independently replicated runtime benchmark.

The practical lesson is simple: a synthetic graph can score close to the original publication under a primary classifier while transferring less faithfully to another model family. Reproduction claims should state which property was reproduced—and under which protocol.
