Vision Transformers for Facial Expression Recognition.

ViT-B/16 vs. TransNeXt-B vs. ShuffViT-DFER on FER-2013: classification accuracy, parameter count, inference latency and webcam throughput.

AidenResearch period: 2025Published: 22 Nov 2026

Research question

When is a smaller vision model the better engineering choice?

Three evaluated architectures provide a concrete example of the gap between parameter capacity and task performance. The evidence is narrow but useful: a compact hybrid led the reported FER-2013 comparison on both classification accuracy and measured latency.

Scope of this retrospective

Survey + reported experiment. Results are taken from the original 2025 report, with implementation context cross-checked against the historical source repository. This is not a new training run or a controlled architectural ablation.

In brief

On FER-2013, the compact hybrid ShuffViT-DFER reached 67.07% top-1 accuracy with 6.22M parameters, ahead of TransNeXt-B (65.60%, 88.96M) and ViT-B/16 (64.93%, 85.80M), and had the lowest measured GPU latency (67 ms).

These are reported results from a 2025 study with non-identical training recipes and no repeated seeds: one accuracy–efficiency comparison, not a general rule for hybrid architectures.

01 / Context

The research question.

01—07

Facial expression recognition creates a useful test bed for comparing local visual processing, long-range feature interaction, and the computational resources needed to combine them.

Convolutional networks are built around local spatial structure. Vision Transformers allow tokens from different image regions to interact through attention. The two representational strategies are complementary, but neither alone guarantees high accuracy on facial expression recognition (FER). Their effectiveness also depends on data, pretraining, class distribution and optimization [1][2][3][6].

A facial image may contain small changes around the mouth and eyes, while the distinction between expression categories may depend on relations between multiple regions. These are reasons to investigate hybrid designs—not evidence that one family must dominate every benchmark.

Question under study

Can a compact hybrid CNN–Transformer configuration achieve a better observed accuracy–efficiency trade-off than substantially larger visual backbones on FER-2013?

The retrospective brings together the architectural literature available in 2025 with the reported results of three configurations: ViT-B/16, TransNeXt-B, and ShuffViT-DFER. It does not claim to introduce a new architecture.

02 / Literature

Three architectural directions.

02—07

The development of efficient vision models is better understood as multiple design directions than as a single sequence in which each architecture replaces the last.

A / FOUNDATIONS

Convolution & patch attention

VGG and ResNet established strong convolutional representations; the original ViT treated image patches as a token sequence processed by Transformer blocks [1][2][3][6].

Local priors ↔ global interaction
B / OPTIMIZED

Richer visual backbones

SwinV2 and TransNeXt reorganized attention and multi-scale processing to improve representation and scaling behavior [14][17].

Capacity ↔ attention design
C / EFFICIENT & HYBRID

Compute-aware combinations

MobileViT, EfficientFormer and TinyViT targeted efficient inference or pretraining; FER-specific models pursued local–global fusion [10][15][16][12][13][18][20].

From local processing to self-attention

ResNet's residual connections made deep convolutional architectures easier to optimize, while retaining the locality and parameter sharing characteristic of CNNs [2]. ViT reframed images as sequences of projected patches. Its ability to model token interactions globally offers a different approach to contextual information, but ordinary global self-attention uses an interaction matrix that grows approximately with the square of token count [3][6].

The trade-off is conditional. A pretrained ViT can be competitive, but its performance on a moderately sized target dataset cannot be inferred from architecture alone. Resolution, initialization and training conditions affect both accuracy and computation.

Optimized attention and more efficient representations

SwinV2 explored scaling through hierarchical representations and shifted-window processing [14]. TransNeXt introduced aggregated attention and convolutional gated linear units (Conv-GLU) to address visual modeling limitations and improve receptive-field behavior [17]. Thus the evaluated TransNeXt-B is best described as an optimized visual backbone, not as a model without convolutional inductive bias.

A separate efficiency-focused line includes MobileViT, EfficientFormer and TinyViT. These papers examine combinations of mobile-oriented operations, architecture design, and distillation or pretraining strategies [10][15][16]. They supply context for the efficiency question even though they were not all directly evaluated in this experiment.

Facial-expression-specific hybrids

Hybrid CNN–Transformer approaches for facial affect and facial-expression analysis include dual-stream processing, convolution–attention fusion and local-attention mechanisms. The ABAW4 hybrid, CT-DBN, HLA-ViT and Swin-FER illustrate different choices of feature extraction and context modeling [12][13][18][19]. The compact ShuffViT-DFER architecture represents the lightweight direction selected for this study [20].

Boundary of the survey

The literature motivates possible advantages of different designs. It does not establish which specific component caused the accuracy differences in our experiment. For that, matched architectural ablations would be required.

03 / Method

A practical three-model comparison.

03—07

The experiment evaluated three established architectures on the same named benchmark, with their reported training configurations retained as historical evidence.

FER-2013: original dataset structure

FER-2013 contains 35,887 grayscale images at 48 × 48 resolution, annotated with seven expression labels: Angry, Disgust, Fear, Happy, Sad, Surprise and Neutral. Its standard partitions consist of 28,709 training, 3,589 public validation and 3,589 test examples.

What the operating prototype adds

Model accuracy is an offline prediction metric. An interactive webcam application must also acquire frames, detect faces, preprocess inputs, perform inference and display the output. The original demonstration reports 11 FPS for ViT-B/16, 7 FPS for TransNeXt-B, and 15 FPS for ShuffViT-DFER in the GPU-assisted webcam pipeline, including face detection.

Measurement boundary

Do not equate published model-only throughput with the application FPS. The original report mentions higher FPS figures in other contexts; the end-to-end GPU webcam measurement reported for the compact hybrid is 15 FPS. The former cannot replace the latter.

These observations support a comparative engineering conclusion: in the recorded environment, the compact configuration achieved the highest reported accuracy, the smallest parameter count and the shortest reported FER latency. They do not establish performance on an untested device or a new deployment stack.

The hybrid leads on Disgust, Fear, Happy and Neutral, while TransNeXt-B leads on Angry and Sad, and ViT-B/16 leads on Surprise. The largest class-level contrast is Disgust: 65.8% for the hybrid against 51.4% for ViT-B/16 and 45.9% for TransNeXt-B.

One plausible hypothesis is that a combination of local processing and efficient attention can preserve informative facial details. The data alone does not isolate that mechanism. Class imbalance, fine-tuning and preprocessing are alternative contributors.

05 / Interpretation

What the results can—and cannot—explain.

05—07

Architecture comparisons reveal observed trade-offs. They do not, by themselves, measure the independent causal contribution of a specific component.

Observation 01

Capacity did not determine the ranking.

Within this three-configuration comparison, the largest parameter counts did not produce the highest reported accuracy. The hybrid used about 14× fewer parameters and remained competitive.

Observation 02

The runtime boundary matters.

GPU/non-GPU latency and webcam FPS describe different operating conditions. Architectural efficiency should be evaluated at the intended application level, not only using one inference figure.

Observation 03

No model dominated every expression.

The compact architecture led the aggregate comparison yet was not best on Angry, Sad or Surprise. A single mean can hide differences that matter to a task.

Observation 04

Architecture was not the only variable.

Model capacity, initialization, training duration and learning rate differ. The result ranks complete evaluated configurations, not isolated design interventions.

What the wider literature contributes

The literature offers mechanisms and techniques worth testing, but the retrospective does not retroactively treat them as part of the 2025 experiment. Class-imbalance-aware losses such as LDAM and data augmentation methods such as CutMix are potential follow-up comparisons, not measured improvements here [4][5].

Self-supervised representations and masked-image pretraining (DINO, MAE and self-supervised Swin approaches) offer another research direction; LoRA-style parameter-efficient adaptation is relevant to the broader adaptation landscape, though no LoRA experiment was reported in the original FER comparison [7][8][9][11].

Why this distinction matters

The role of references is to explain the architectural and optimization landscape available at the time—not to imply that all cited methods were implemented, compared or demonstrated in this experiment.

06 / Evidence boundary

What holds up.

06—07

Limitations of the retrospective

Single dataset

The empirical comparison concerns FER-2013. It cannot establish performance across other facial datasets or unconstrained video environments.

Non-identical training recipes

Learning rates, epochs, batch sizes and architecture initialization differ. The results compare complete configurations, not controlled single-factor ablations.

Point estimates without paired uncertainty

Repeated seeds, paired confidence intervals and significance analysis were not provided. Differences of one or two percentage points require cautious interpretation.

Historical implementation and hardware

The repository provides code context, but this retrospective did not independently rerun the full experiments or reconstruct every original runtime environment.

Observed facial expressions are not internal emotions

The classifier estimates dataset expression categories from image appearance. It does not directly measure a person's subjective emotional state.

Prototype is not deployment validation

The FastAPI/React demonstration shows technical feasibility and relative runtime behavior, not production-grade performance, privacy, reliability or robustness.

Retrospective conclusion

In this comparison, the compact hybrid wins.

ShuffViT-DFER led all three evaluated configurations on accuracy and measured efficiency. It reached 67.07% Top-1 accuracy versus 65.60% for TransNeXt-B and 64.93% for ViT-B/16, while using just 6.22M parameters instead of approximately 86–89M. It also recorded the lowest inference latency in both reported hardware settings.

The outcome is a clear accuracy–efficiency win for this FER-2013 experiment. It does not by itself establish that every hybrid architecture will win, or identify which individual component caused the difference.

Questions worth revisiting

Future work could compare models under matched pretraining and training budgets, introduce explicit component ablations, report uncertainty across repeated runs, and standardize hardware measurements at both model-only and end-to-end levels. Those experiments were not part of the 2025 results.
