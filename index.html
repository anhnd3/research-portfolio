02 / ABOUT ME

Curiosity,
with a method.

I'm Aiden, a software engineer interested in both the practical and research sides of artificial intelligence. I recently defended my master's thesis in AI, examining the validity of counterfactual explanations and how natural-language user feedback can be translated into structured constraints.

My questions often begin with something practical: Does a model improve on a simpler baseline? Under which conditions does an improvement hold? And what can an average benchmark score hide? I explore these questions across explainable and causal AI, information retrieval, compact models, and graph-based methods.

I learn from the literature, reproduce existing work, and use diagnostic experiments and matched comparisons where possible. Not every experiment isolates a cause, and not every result generalizes. This portfolio records what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and which questions are worth revisiting.

03 / PUBLICATION POLICY

Evidence before claims.

How sources, experiments, claims and corrections are handled across different types of work.

01 / INTERPRETATION

Scope every claim.

Separate literature-based claims from original experiments. For measurements, disclose cohorts, controls, denominators and limitations; avoid unsupported causal attribution.

02 / REPRODUCIBILITY

Keep the audit trail.

Document methodology, references and revisions. A study appearing here does not mean its supporting dataset, model checkpoints or coursework source files are publicly released.

03 / CORRECTIONS

Invite scrutiny.

Corrections and technical questions belong in the GitHub repository. No automatic open-source license or third-party reuse permission is implied.
